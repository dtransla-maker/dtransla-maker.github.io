How we work

We join your team, inside your program, and deliver the work.

One service where your team is thin, or development, quality, regulatory, testing, and cybersecurity coordinated as one team.

Book a 30-minute callSee the Integrated Submission Program

One plan

Five functions work from one submission plan.

Requirements, code, risk files, test evidence, and cybersecurity documentation get planned against what your submission needs to claim from day one, not reconciled at the end.

  1. 01Development

    Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) and embedded software, built with IEC 62304 lifecycle records.

  2. 02Quality

    ISO 13485 quality management system (QMS) and ISO 14971 risk management, implemented with your team.

  3. 03Regulatory

    Software classification, 510(k) or De Novo submission, Design History File (DHF) and submission documentation.

  4. 04Testing

    Verification and validation, planned against what your submission claims.

  5. 05Cybersecurity

    Threat modeling, risk analysis, Software Bill of Materials (SBOM), and premarket cybersecurity documentation, including your security testing evidence, designed to support compliance with IEC 81001-5-1.

The handoffs

Every handoff is mapped, from one function to the next.

The gaps sit between teams, so that is where we put the coordination. We map what each function owes the next, and raise blockers before they reach your desk.

  1. DevelopmentQuality

    Requirements, code, and IEC 62304 records written as one set, not reconciled at the end.

  2. RegulatoryTesting

    Verification and validation evidence planned against each claim your submission makes about the software.

  3. QualityRegulatory

    The Design History File (DHF) holds every document the submission points to, in its current version.

  4. TestingCybersecurity

    Threat model, cybersecurity risk assessment, Software Bill of Materials (SBOM), and testing documentation that trace to each other, as FDA recommends.

  5. CybersecurityDevelopment

    Threat model updated when the architecture changes.

FDA recommends this traceability in "Cybersecurity in Medical Devices: Quality Management System Considerations and Content of Premarket Submissions" (final guidance, February 3, 2026).
